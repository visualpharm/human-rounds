Care moves. The record stays authoritative.

Sana automates the work between a patient’s first contact and the existing clinical record. It does not ask a public provider to replace its core system.

What exists today

Sana is a collection of tested prototypes, not a finished medical device. Each capability is labelled by its real status.

Working prototypes

Implemented and available in the public demo or the Argentine reference projects.

  • Patient identity capture from an Argentine identity card, typed data or a photo.
  • Mobile self-registration with reception confirmation before the patient enters the health record.
  • Voice-first intake that structures the patient’s own narrative without overwriting answers.
  • Queues, referrals and appointments connected to the patient journey.
  • Deterministic red-flag rules that an artificial-intelligence model cannot downgrade.
  • Health-record handoff to Argentina’s Integrated Health Record in the local reference deployment.
Clinical models in development

Implemented in the demonstration environment and now moving through validation, governance and specialty expansion.

  • Clinician summaries that show the most consequential information first.
  • Evidence-backed differential diagnosis presented as suggestions for professional review.
  • Probability estimates only after calibration with the population and setting where they will be used.
  • Suggested examinations constrained to locally approved catalogues and protocols.
  • Additional specialty models beyond the current cardiology prototype.

No model diagnoses, prescribes, discharges or changes care without a professional action.

Open by design

Public health should not become dependent on another closed platform. Sana is being separated into reusable modules and country connectors.

Open source

The public repository starts with the roadmap, architecture and website source. Reviewed application modules will follow.

Local control

Deploy locally or in a chosen cloud. Institutions keep authority over clinical records and operating policies.

Interoperable

Country connectors translate Sana workflows into the national or institutional systems already in use.

Resilient

Shared devices, intermittent connectivity and low-bandwidth workflows are first-class design conditions.

What grant funding unlocks

Grant support turns the prototypes into audited, reusable public infrastructure and proves their value in real clinical operations.

  1. 01

    Pilot deployment

    Configure, train and support a real public-health site with synthetic rehearsal before patient use.

  2. 02

    Local integration

    Connect identity, referrals, appointments and records to the systems authorised in that jurisdiction.

  3. 03

    Independent validation

    Measure time saved, access, safety, clinician workload and model calibration with an external evaluation partner.

  4. 04

    Open implementation kit

    Publish adapters, operating guides, governance templates and findings so the next site starts further ahead.

Grant-funded pilots can launch without a software licence fee for participating public providers. Funding pays for implementation, integration, validation and support.

Built in Argentina. Designed to travel.

The first reference connects municipal appointment and care workflows to Argentina’s Integrated Health Record. The same operating layer can connect to another country’s equivalent instead of rebuilding its clinical record.

  • Latin America: national or provincial health-information networks and public hospital systems.
  • Sub-Saharan Africa: offline-first registration, community-health workflows and referral exchange.
  • South and Southeast Asia: national identity, consent and health-information exchange platforms.
  • Higher-regulation markets: non-clinical workflow modules first; clinical models only through the applicable medical-device pathway.
